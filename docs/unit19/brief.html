1. Assignment guide

Unit 19 is assessed by two assignments set by your teacher. This page explains what each task asks, in plain English, and what a strong answer includes. Always follow your teacher’s official brief and deadlines.

What Pass, Merit and Distinction mean

GradeCommand wordWhat the assessor looks for
PassExplain, design, develop, test, reviewCorrect, detailed explanations and complete, working evidence. Some small inaccuracies are allowed, and your review may be one-sided.
MeritAnalyse, justify, optimiseYou show how and why: components are analysed in depth, design decisions are backed by requirements and technical reasons, and you improve the network based on your tests. Technically accurate throughout.
DistinctionEvaluate, demonstrateBalanced judgements supported by evidence and reasoned examples, reaching justified conclusions and future recommendations. Fluent technical vocabulary and connected chains of reasoning. For BC.D3, clear evidence that you managed yourself and the project throughout.

Meet every Pass criterion first. Merit and Distinction build on the same work: they are about the depth of your thinking, not the length.

Assignment 1: Investigating computer networks

Learning aim A. You are a junior network technician at a company that designs and installs networks. The company publishes detailed technical pages on its website to help new and existing customers understand networks.

Hand in: A report (written as website pages and a table)

CriterionWhat the task asks (in plain English)Checklist: a strong answer…Help
A.P1Website pages: why different network types and models are needed
Explain why organisations need each network type (LAN, WLAN, WAN, SAN, intranet, extranet, internet, cloud, wired and wireless integration) and how those needs affect the choice of network model (peer-to-peer, client/server, thin client). Include why protocols and standards matter, why different topologies are used, and current trends such as BYOD and cloud computing.
  • Every network type is explained with a clear reason why an organisation would need it
  • Each type has a realistic example organisation or situation
  • Peer-to-peer, client/server and thin client are explained, and you show how an organisation's needs decide which model it chooses
  • Topologies are covered, with why different ones are used
  • You explain why protocols and standards (e.g. IEEE 802, TCP/IP) are needed to connect different networks
  • Current trends are included: at least BYOD and cloud, ideally virtualisation and SDN too
  • It is written for a customer reading a website: clear headings, plain explanations of technical terms
  • It is in your own words, with any sources referenced

A.D1Report: evaluating network models for different clients
Write a clear and balanced evaluation of peer-to-peer, client/server and thin client models and their suitability for different client requirements. Consider ease of use, ease of set-up, performance and suitability for different kinds of applications, with reasoned examples, benefits and drawbacks.

Assignment 2: Design, build, test and evaluate a network

Learning aims B and C. You are a junior network engineer. Your supervisor has asked you to design, install, test and evaluate a network for a small branch office of a customer that creates web designs.

The client’s requirements

  • A server providing DHCP, DNS and web services
  • At least 10 workstations
  • A wired network plus secure Wi-Fi
  • Wireless laptops at both sites
  • Internet access, and a router connection between both sites (one off-site office)
  • Suitable networking equipment, and an inventory of it
  • Users and groups with folder permissions as specified in the brief's table (HR, ADMIN and IT_SERVICES groups; you own all folders)
  • Scalable and secure for the future
  • Static addressing, DHCP or both
  • Low cost with reasonable performance
  • VoIP
  • Antivirus
  • Facilities for e-commerce
  • All network devices password protected
  • Full documentation including test plans and results

Your teacher’s brief has the full office layout and the users, groups and permissions table.

Hand in: Customer email; design documentation; written justification; implementation evidence (screenshots, photos); test evidence; evaluation; self-management portfolio

CriterionWhat the task asks (in plain English)Checklist: a strong answer…Help
B.P3Email to the customer: why design and planning come first
Write a professional email to the customer explaining why a network design and planning strategies are needed before installation, and how they make sure the network meets the organisation's needs and design goals.

  • Explains the purpose of a network design and of planning strategies
  • Links design and planning to the customer's needs and business goals

  • Explains the risks of installing without a plan
  • Clear, polite tone with technical terms explained

Your own work, and using AI properly

BTEC assignments must be your own work. You sign a declaration saying so. Copying from the internet, another student or an AI tool, even if you change the words, is malpractice and can mean losing the unit.

  • Allowed: asking the AI tutor or coach to explain an idea, quiz you, check what a task is asking, help you plan, or tell you what is missing from your draft.
  • Not allowed: asking any AI to write paragraphs, tables, emails, designs or evaluations for you, or copying AI wording into your work.
  • Be open: the Assignment builder keeps an AI-use log of every question you ask the coach. Download it and hand it in, and tell your teacher how AI helped you.
  • Do not put personal details (yours or anyone else’s) into the tutor.
